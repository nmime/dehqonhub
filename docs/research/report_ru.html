1. Исполнительное резюме

Uzbekistan's AgriTech ecosystem comprises approximately 60+ identifiable players across startups, government platforms, banks, NGOs, and research institutions. The ecosystem is government-led with strong donor backing (World Bank, IFAD, FAO, UNDP, USAID). Key private platforms include UFarmer (12,000+ farmers, 850+ sellers), AgroHub (largest agri-input marketplace), and AgroMart. The government operates a centralized Digital Agriculture platform (integratsion.agro.uz) integrating 20+ departmental systems. Drone/satellite monitoring is rapidly expanding with Kaizen Aerospace (300,000 ha MoU), Meraque ($4.5M manufacturing investment), and OneSoil (BCG-mediated satellite mapping). Agricultural insurance was launched in April 2025 via UNDP/Swiss Re/Europa RE with Uzagrosugurta and Semurg as local partners. The AgriTech4Uzbekistan Innovation Challenge (CGIAR) attracted 571 applications from 11 countries, selecting 22 teams including 10 accelerators receiving $30K equity-free grants. As of mid-2025, Uzum serves 17M+ users monthly with $500M GMV and is the country's first tech unicorn. A new AI-equipped Samarkand-2028 satellite for agricultural monitoring was announced December 2025. No dedicated cold-chain technology startups were identified; cold chain development has been driven by GCCA/USAID training programs since 2011.

2. Размер рынка и сегменты

Uzbekistan's agricultural sector produced 444.6 trillion soums ($34.2B) in 2024, up from 426.3 trillion soums ($34.8B) in 2023. The sector accounts for approximately 19% of GDP and employs about 24-25% of the labor force, though employment share has been declining for 5 consecutive years. Key outputs include 9M tons of grain, 3M+ tons of cotton, 16M+ tons of vegetables/melons, 5M tons of fruits, and 4M tons of potatoes (all 2024). Livestock stands at 14.28M cattle (Oct 2025), 24.81M sheep/goats, and 110.8M poultry heads. Agricultural exports reached $2.175B in 2024 with Russia, Pakistan, and Kazakhstan as top destinations. The sector is dominated by dehkan and subsidiary farms (63.1% of output in 2024), mechanization rose from 67% to 81% between 2018-2023, and arable land totals approximately 4.47M hectares with roughly 3.3-4.3M hectares irrigated.

Конкурентные пробелы

  • Нет сквозной платформы: UFarmer leads on farmer side but lacks output aggregation. AgroHub focuses only on inputs.
  • Правительственные платформы изолированы: Digital Agriculture integrates 20+ systems but serves as admin portal, not commercial marketplace.
  • Пробел в холодовой цепи: Only 7% coverage, no dedicated cold-chain tech startups.
  • Фрагментированная консультация: Dehqon.uz does disease detection, Qidirpotizlik does precision irrigation — no unified advisory layer.

4. Анализ цепочек поставок

Uzbekistan's agriculture sector accounts for ~19% of GDP and employs ~24% of the labor force. In 2024 it produced 9 Mt grain, ~3 Mt cotton, 16 Mt vegetables/melons, 5 Mt fruits, 4 Mt potatoes, 2.9 Mt meat, and 12.4 Mt milk (stat.uz, USDA GAIN Dec 2025). The supply chain is characterized by high intermediary fragmentation, limited cold chain infrastructure (1.14 Mt capacity covering only 4.9% of fruit/veg output), post-harvest losses of 20-30% for perishables, and a state-influenced pricing system operating through the Uzbek Commodity Exchange (UzEx). Contract farming exists primarily within cotton-textile clusters (134 clusters covering ~88% of cotton area). Forward pricing operates via UzEx futures for cotton (UZS 7.8mn/ton in 2024) and wheat. Seed import dependency stands at 45% ($182M in 2025). Agricultural lending rates average 23.13% with preferential state-subsidized rates available. Only 15% of fruits/vegetables are processed; cold storage capacity must grow 33.8% to support current export levels.

  • WHEAT CHAIN (Farm→Elevator→Mill→Bakery/Retailer→Consumer): Farmgate price $0.26/kg (FAOSTAT/Selina Wamucii, May 2026); 2024 production 6.73 Mt wheat (FAOSTAT); state switched to market prices June 2022 after years of fixed-price procurement (USDA GAIN). Bread prices rose 77.6% cumulatively over 5 years (CBU study, May 2025). Typical intermediaries: collection centers/elevators (margin ~5-8%), mills (margin ~10-15%), bakeries/retailers (margin ~20-30%). Farmer share of final bread price estimated at 15-25%. Number of intermediaries: 3-4.
  • COTTON CHAIN (Farm→Ginnery→Textile Mill Cluster→Exporter): 2024 production ~1 Mt lint equivalent (World Bank); 2023 FAOSTAT reports 810.6 kt. Commodity exchange futures price set at UZS 7.8mn ($611)/ton for 2024 with UZS 1mn ($78)/ton subsidy (GLEAF/Cabinet of Ministers, Sep 2024). 134 cotton-textile clusters operate (Better Cotton Initiative, 2024). Average yarn export price $2,901/MT in 2024, down 5% YoY (USDA GAIN Cotton Annual 2026). Clusters integrate ginning, spinning, weaving. Manual labor constitutes significant portion of production costs; mechanization lagging (economist Muzaffar Razakov, GLEAF Sep 2024). Farmer receives commodity exchange price minus cluster deductions; production costs exceed global prices creating market failure (Razakov). Intermediary margin at ginning stage ~5-10%; spinning adds ~15-25%; fabric/export adds ~20-35%. Number of intermediaries: 2-3 (cluster model consolidates).
  • TOMATO/VEGETABLE CHAIN (Farm→Collector→Transporter→Wholesale Bazaar→Retailer→Consumer): Farmgate price $0.73/kg (FAOSTAT/Selina Wamucii); 2024 production 2.3 Mt (FAOSTAT); 2024 exports 48.5 kt valued at $46.4M at avg $0.96/kg (UN Comtrade). Only 10-15% of vegetables processed (USDA GAIN Food Processing Dec 2025). Post-harvest losses 20-30% (UzDaily/FreshPlaza Oct 2025). Dalatek cooperative model: one supermarket chain needs 60 tons/day domestically; 20-ton shipment = $40,000 turnover (FreshPlaza). Collector margin ~10-15%, transporter ~5-8%, wholesale bazaar ~10-15%, retailer ~20-30%. Farmer share of retail price estimated at 25-35%. Number of intermediaries: 4-5.
  • FRUIT CHAIN (Farm→Packhouse→Exporter or Wholesale Bazaar→Retailer→Consumer): Apple farmgate $0.58/kg; cherry farmgate $0.67/kg (FAOSTAT/Selina Wamucii). 2024 cherry exports: 32.2 kt, $70.4M to 16 countries, avg export price $2.19/kg (UN Comtrade). Cherry FCA export price $3.20-$4.20/kg for 20-26mm caliber (CherryTimes/EastFruit, May 2025). 2024 apple exports: 44.3 kt, $14.5M, avg $0.33/kg (UN Comtrade). 2024 total fruit/veg exports: 2.04 Mt (UzDaily Oct 2025). Russia takes ~70% of cherry exports; Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan are top apple destinations (Kursiv Media Jan 2026). Packhouse/exporter margin ~15-25%; domestic wholesale/retail margin ~20-30%. Farmer share of export cherry price: ~15-25% ($0.67 farmgate vs $2.19 export unit value). Number of intermediaries: 2-3 for export, 3-5 for domestic.
  • WHOLESALE BAZAARS: Chorsu Bazaar (Tashkent) is the historic central market; Bektemir Wholesale Market is the largest agricultural wholesale hub serving Tashkent metro area. Specific daily visitor counts and transaction volumes not available in public sources. Regional yarmarkas (weekly agricultural fairs) operate across all regions but no official count or volume data published. AgroIndex/UFarmer platform tracks daily wholesale vegetable/fruit prices from Tashkent and regional bazaars (uzagroindex.uz).
  • POST-HARVEST LOSSES: 20-30% for fruits and vegetables (UzDaily/FreshPlaza Oct 2025; USDA GAIN Food Processing). 30% lost due to insufficient storage and processing capacity in 2023 (USDA GAIN). Specific crop-by-crop rates: tomatoes ~25-30%, apples ~20-25%, melons/watermelons ~25-30%, grapes ~20-25%, other vegetables ~20-30% (expert estimates cited by UzDaily). Reducing losses could generate additional $1.1-1.7B for farms (UzDaily Oct 2025).
  • COLD CHAIN INFRASTRUCTURE: As of Oct 1, 2025: 2,011 cold storage facilities, total capacity 1.14 Mt (UzDaily Oct 2025). By region: Samarkand 184.8 kt (16.2%), Ferghana 168.2 kt (14.7%), Tashkent 129.6 kt (11.4%). Facility size distribution: 50.3% medium (100-1,000t), 46.3% large (>1,000t), 3.4% small (<100t). Covers only 4.9% of total annual fruit/veg output. Coverage by region: highest Tashkent 6.9%, Ferghana 6.4%, Kashkadarya 6.4%; lowest Syrdarya 2.0%, Bukhara 2.5%, Karakalpakstan 3.5%. Current deficit vs export need: 386 kt shortfall (33.8% increase needed). Government target: expand to 1.93 Mt by 2027 (Presidential Resolution PP-136, Apr 2025). Refrigerated truck count: not publicly reported; Chinese manufacturers exporting ISUZU refrigerated trucks in batches (Chengli Group). Cold chain handling penetration estimated at <10% of total produce.
  • EXPORT LOGISTICS: Top destinations Russia (~70% cherries), Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Belarus, UAE, China. Customs clearance time: not specifically quantified for agricultural goods; cross-border Kazakhstan-Uzbekistan corridor has documented discrepancies in documentation requirements, tariff classification, and inspection frequency (gettransport.com). Phytosanitary certification: government targeting phytosanitary permits for 30 new product types to 8 countries by end 2025 (USDA GAIN PP-136). Freight costs per ton to key destinations: not publicly quantified; cherry shipments under FCA terms.
  • CONTRACT FARMING: 63.1% of total agricultural production comes from dehkan (smallholder) and household farms (trend.az/stat.uz 2024). Cotton: nearly universal contract arrangement through 134 clusters controlling ~88% of cotton area (World Bank 2020; Better Cotton Initiative 2024). Potato: empirical study of 180 smallholder potato farmers shows contract farming active, with price, payment method, input provision, and buyer relationship being most important attributes (Sanaev et al., Green Economy & Development journal, 2024). No precise national percentage for contract farming prevalence by crop type beyond cotton (near-universal via clusters).
  • FORWARD PRICING MECHANISMS: Uzbek Commodity Exchange (UzEx - uzex.uz) operates futures contracts for cotton and wheat. Cotton futures price set at UZS 7.8mn ($611)/ton for 2024 with minimum exchange price UZS 6.8mn ($533)/ton (Cabinet of Ministers/GLEAF Sep 2024). Wheat switched to market-based exchange pricing from June 2022 (USDA GAIN). Prices discovered through UzEx trading sessions with weekly quotes published. Economists have proposed price corridors and futures indices but minimum-price model remains dominant (Razakov, GLEAF Sep 2024).
  • SEED SUPPLY CHAIN: Import dependency 45% ($182M total imports in 2025, +11.3% YoY). Total annual demand ~68,000 tonnes including farm-saved seeds; commercial market ~31,000 tonnes. Domestic commercial output ~17,000 tonnes; imports ~14,000 tonnes (IBI CrossBorder July 2026). China is second-largest supplier at 22.3% market share; China-Uzbekistan seed exports Jan-May 2026: $28.6M (+17.8% YoY). Supply chain: breeder (international/domestic) → multiplier/importer → wholesaler/distributor → agro-dealer → farmer. Markup structure: breeder to importer ~30-50%, importer to wholesaler ~20-30%, wholesaler to agro-dealer ~15-25%, agro-dealer to farmer ~25-40%. Total markup from breeder to farmer estimated at 100-200%+. Key suppliers include Seitec Genetics (US corn), Electus/Bravo (Turkish), Agrobest group (via Sam Agro Center distributor).
  • FERTILIZER SUPPLY CHAIN: NPK fertilizer import price $1,239/ton in 2024 (-19.4% YoY) (IndexBox). Chain: manufacturer (mostly imported from China, Russia, Turkey, Iran) → port/border warehouse → national distributor → regional agro-dealer → farmer. Major distributors: Petrochem Agro (est. 2016), AGRO BIZNES EKSPORT LLC (est. 2007), Sam Agro Center. Average markup from factory gate to farmer: port-to-distributor ~15-20%, distributor-to-agro-dealer ~20-30%, agro-dealer-to-farmer ~25-40%. Total markup estimated at 60-100%+ from factory gate to farmer. Urea prill/granular prices tracked at urea-prices.com for Uzbekistan.

5. Профиль фермера

Uzbekistan's rural population stands at approximately 18.1-18.9 million (49.3-50.3% of 36.7-37.0 million total as of early-mid 2024). Agriculture employs 25.65% of the national labor force (2024, World Bank ILO estimate), down from 39.53% in 1994 — a structural decline of 14 percentage points over three decades. The country's farming structure is dominated by smallholder dehkan farms: 95% of all farms are dehkan operations averaging under 0.2 hectares each, yet they produce 60-70% of all agricultural output and own 93% of cattle. Remittances reached a record $14.8 billion in 2024 (Central Bank of Uzbekistan), with Russia contributing 77% ($11.5B). Digital infrastructure is advanced for the region: 89% internet penetration nationally (DataReportal 2025), 92.2% mobile connection coverage, and Click alone has exceeded 18.4 million registered users (Oct 2024). However, the rural-urban digital divide persists sharply: only 48% of rural residents use the internet vs. 83% urban (ITU Facts & Figures 2024). Smartphone ownership in remote areas reaches 80-84% (UNDP GDDA 2023). Telegram dominates as the primary information channel with 76% reach among internet users. The FAO Digital Villages Initiative has piloted IoT-based smart greenhouse systems in Novkent and Yuksalish villages in Fergana Valley. Key barriers to AgriTech adoption include low digital literacy among older farmers, fragmented small plot sizes (0.2-0.5 ha), limited relevance of existing apps to smallholder needs, and cost sensitivity given that agriculture contributes only 10.7% of household income.

Twiga Foods raised $110M+ across 25 investors but underwent 'Project Easter' restructuring with 300+ layoffs and pivot to FMCG. WayCool raised $303-388M total but executed three layoff rounds (70+200+ employees) due to asset-heavy cold chain model. Telegram Mini Apps offer CAC of $0.02-0.50/user vs $2-10+ for native apps — a 40-500x advantage critical for Central Asia where Telegram has near-universal penetration.

9. Государственная поддержка и программы

Uzbekistan has undergone the most comprehensive agricultural transformation in Central Asia since 2017, with President Mirziyoyev issuing dozens of decrees abolishing cotton quotas, restructuring financing, creating 52+ subsidy types (consolidating to 75 total programs under a new Payments Agency from Nov 2025), launching a unified Digital Agriculture platform (July 2025), deploying satellite monitoring across 4M+ hectares via Uzbekcosmos, establishing preferential lending at 14% through PP-113, targeting $3.5B in fruit/vegetable exports by 2025, securing $475M+ in World Bank/ADB/IFAD financing, and creating the ANORA platform targeting $1.5B in agrifood investment by 2030. The sector accounts for 20% of GDP (467 trillion UZS production), employs 3.5M workers, and represents one-third of exports. Total agricultural spending reached 29 trillion soums in financing plus 2.35 trillion in subsidies in 2025, with 34.2 trillion planned for 2026.

10. Стратегия PTA (President Tech Award)

The President Tech Award (PTA), Uzbekistan's flagship startup competition, has evolved from a $1M prize fund in 2023-2024 to a planned $5M fund by 2026 under Resolution PP-59. It now operates three tracks: Incubation ($40K grants for 25 winners, 2-5 person teams at idea/MVP stage), Acceleration ($100K investments for 15 winners with PMF and 3+ months of sales), and Best Startup Project ($125K/$75K/$50K prizes across 10 categories including AI, Enterprise, DeepTech, HealthTech, GovTech). For an AgriTech B2B startup targeting the Incubation track, the winning formula requires: a working MVP (not just slides), a team of 3-5 members combining technical and domain expertise, clear alignment with national priorities (food security, export growth, digital transformation per Resolution PP-59), and demonstrable early validation through pilot customers or LOIs. Winning teams consistently show: functional products validated by real users, international jury-ready presentations, government-aligned social impact narratives, and clear paths to commercialization. The evaluation criteria explicitly prioritize: relevance to national challenges, competitive advantage over existing solutions, market demand/commercial prospects, product completeness, team composition, presentation quality, and technical architecture/scalability.

Стратегические последствия

  • FOR TRACK SELECTION: AgriTech B2B should target either Incubation track (if at MVP stage, team 3-5 people) or Best Startup Project > Enterprise category (if Seed stage with some traction). Acceleration track requires 3+ months of actual sales — likely premature for MVP-stage AgriTech.
  • FOR MVP READINESS: Judges conduct explicit fact-checking of product functionality. A clickable prototype is insufficient — the product must demonstrably work. Budget time to build a functional MVP that handles core AgriTech workflows before applying.
  • FOR TEAM COMPOSITION: Winning teams average 5-15 members. For Incubation (2-5 required), aim for exactly 4-5 members: technical founder + agribusiness domain expert + design/product person + sales/business development. Domain expertise in agriculture is critical differentiator.
  • FOR PITCH CONTENT: Structure around the 7 evaluation criteria explicitly. Lead with relevance to Uzbekistan's food security and agricultural modernization priorities (Resolution PP-59). Show competitive advantage vs. existing solutions (manual processes, fragmented supply chains). Demonstrate market demand through pilot LOIs or farmer/agricultural enterprise interviews.
  • FOR GOVERNMENT ALIGNMENT: Frame as advancing Resolution PP-59 goals: digital transformation of agriculture, export growth, youth employment. Reference specific national programs. Position as enabling the government's stated goal of 5,000 active startups and $2B VC attraction by 2030.
  • FOR INTERNATIONAL COMPARABLES: Use DeHaat, Ninjacart, OrdaAgro as proof points carefully — show how your model adapts to Uzbekistan's specific context (state farms, irrigation systems, cotton/wheat focus, Central Asian supply chains). Emphasize localization, not copying.
  • FOR FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS: Given KPI-based tranche payments, projections must be specific and achievable within 12 months. Focus on: number of pilot customers converted to paying, revenue milestones, user growth metrics. The 50%/25%/25% payment structure means judges will scrutinize whether you can hit realistic KPIs.
  • FOR DEMO PREPARATION: Prepare a live demo showing core functionality end-to-end. Test thoroughly — fact-checking stage eliminates teams whose products don't work. Have backup video demo in case of technical issues during live presentation.
